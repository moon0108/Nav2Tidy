# nav — A* + MPPI navigation for the TidyBot++ base

Implements PLAN.md. Pure numpy, base only, no changes to the existing repo files.

```bash
python -m nav.runner --start 0 0 --goal 8 8                 # viewer, real time
python -m nav.runner --start 0 0 --goal 8 8 --no-viewer --fast
python -m pytest nav/tests -q
```

## Pre-work findings (PLAN section 1)

1. **`action['base_pose']` is an absolute global pose.** `BaseController.control_callback()`
   (mujoco_env.py:129) assigns it straight to `otg_inp.target_position`, whose output drives the
   `joint_x` / `joint_y` / `joint_th` position actuators in `base.xml`. Not a delta.
2. **Arm/gripper actions can be omitted** — `ArmController` only reacts to the `arm_pos` /
   `gripper_pos` keys. But `MujocoSim` itself cannot be reused: it hardcodes `pinch_site`,
   `gen3/base_link` and the cube qpos slice, none of which exist in a base-only scene. `NavSim`
   (nav/runner.py) wraps MuJoCo directly and reuses only `BaseController`, so the base still moves
   under the same Ruckig limits as the real robot.
3. **`get_obs()` returns no velocity** (`base_pose`, `arm_pos`, `arm_quat`, `gripper_pos`). MPPI is
   a pure integrator and only needs the pose; metrics finite-difference the pose for speed/jerk.

## Deviations from PLAN, and why

| PLAN | Built | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `models/nav_scene.xml` | `models/stanford_tidybot/nav_scene.xml` | `base.xml`'s `meshdir="../assets"` resolves relative to the scene file, so a scene one directory up cannot find the meshes. Generated by `nav/scene.py`, never hand-edited. |
| `env.step({'base_pose': x + u*dt})` | `x + u * 1.5` | The OTG plans to *stop* at the target, so a target one control period ahead caps the base at 0.14 m/s. 1.5 s of lead is this repo's own idiom for velocity-to-position commands (gamepad_teleop.py:71). Restores the full 0.5 m/s. |
| A* on the 0.05 m grid | A* on a 0.10 m grid (`plan_grid()`) | 8-connected A* over 220x220 cells costs up to 130 ms, more than one control period (PLAN section 9). At 0.10 m it is 30 ms and the path is 0.2 % longer (13.54 m vs 13.51 m). The MPPI cost field stays at 0.05 m. |
| A* clearance = robot radius | `PLAN_CLEARANCE = 0.45` | Planning at exactly 0.35 m leaves no room for tracking error; the base grazed the doorway. 0.45 m still fits the 1.2 m doorway. |
| `lookahead = 1.5` | `0.75` | Swept over the six acceptance pairs: 0.75 gives 6/6 with zero wall contacts, 1.25-1.5 cuts the doorway corner (min clearance 0.25-0.30 m). |
| Gaussian cost-volume kernel | `exp(-(d - 0.65) / decay)`, the `Grid.bake()` shape anchored on contact distance | A Gaussian on the pedestrian centre is only ~0.1 of its peak at the distance that matters and never beat a 0.50 collision rate. Details and the two rejected alternatives are below. |
| densities 3 levels (unspecified) + 0.6 m/s pedestrians | 2/4/8 pedestrians, 0.5 m/s | Pedestrians faster than the base's 0.5 m/s cap can simply run it down, and 8 in the 28.5 m^2 crowd area is 3-7x the density crowd-navigation benchmarks use. |

`nav_mppi.py`'s algorithms are unchanged; they were only split into `grid.py` / `astar.py` /
`mppi.py`, with the MPPI constructor arguments gathered into `MPPIConfig`.

## Phase 3: what the cost volume kernel had to be

PLAN section 6 specifies a Gaussian stamped on each predicted pedestrian position, with sigma
growing over the horizon. Measured at 8 pedestrians x 4 seeds, that model never got the collision
rate below 0.50, and the reason is visible in the numbers: a Gaussian centred on the pedestrian
puts nearly all of its mass where the robot cannot be anyway. At the distance that actually
matters -- contact, 0.65 m = robot radius + pedestrian radius -- a sigma=0.3 kernel is only ~0.1
of its peak, so `w_dyn * cv` never outweighs `w_goal * distance`. Raising `w_dyn` to compensate
made the field wide as well as tall, and the robot detoured until it timed out (success 0.00 at
`w_dyn=300`).

A flat disk with a Gaussian taper was worse still (collision rate 1.00): the plateau removes the
gradient exactly where the robot needs one, and MPPI cannot descend a flat cost.

What works is the shape `Grid.bake()` already uses for static obstacles, anchored on the contact
distance: `exp(-(d - radius) / decay)`, clipped to [0, 1]. It is 1.0 at contact, has a real
gradient everywhere outside it, and stays narrow. Uncertainty growth is expressed by inflating
`radius` and `decay` with the prediction lead instead of by widening a Gaussian.

Two scenario parameters were wrong as well, and no amount of planner tuning would have fixed them:

- **Pedestrian speed.** 0.6 m/s against a base capped at 0.5 m/s means a pedestrian can run the
  robot down; a holonomic sidestep only works if you can outpace the closing motion. Real crowd
  robots move at roughly human speed, so pedestrians now walk at the robot's 0.5 m/s.
- **Density.** 8 pedestrians in the 28.5 m^2 crowd area is 0.28 per m^2, three to seven times the
  0.04-0.1 per m^2 typical of crowd navigation benchmarks. Densities are now 2/4/8, so the top of
  the range is the stress case rather than the nominal one.

### `w_dyn` sweep (4 pedestrians, 5 seeds, `python -m nav.sweep --mppi w_dyn=0,30,60,120`)

| `w_dyn` | success | collision rate | collisions | min distance | nav time | freezing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (blind) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 8 | 0.04 m | 11.6 s | 0.1 s |
| 30 | 1.00 | 0.20 | 1 | 0.16 m | 17.7 s | 0.1 s |
| **60** | **1.00** | **0.00** | **0** | **0.82 m** | **24.8 s** | **0.1 s** |
| 120 | 0.80 | 0.00 | 0 | 0.95 m | 50.4 s | 1.4 s |

`w_dyn=60` -- PLAN's own verified default -- is the knee: 120 buys no further safety and starts
freezing and timing out. Avoidance costs about 2x the nav time of the blind planner.

## Phase 3 acceptance: 3 densities x 20 seeds x 5 variants = 300 episodes

`python -m nav.benchmark --seeds 20 --out nav/results/benchmark.csv`, social-force pedestrians
that do not see the robot. Collision rate is the fraction of episodes with at least one contact.

| pedestrians (per m^2) | variant | success | collision rate | min distance | nav time | mean jerk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (0.07) | baseline (blind) | 1.00 | 0.60 | 0.07 m | 11.6 s | 3.00 |
| 2 | **cost volume** | 0.85 | **0.05** | 0.17 m | 29.1 s | 3.07 |
| 2 | latency 0.15 s | 0.90 | 0.15 | 0.19 m | 26.1 s | 3.08 |
| 2 | latency 0.15 s, uncompensated | 0.95 | 0.15 | 0.47 m | 25.3 s | 3.12 |
| 4 (0.14) | baseline (blind) | 1.00 | 0.85 | 0.04 m | 11.6 s | 3.00 |
| 4 | **cost volume** | 0.70 | **0.20** | 0.46 m | 53.8 s | 3.07 |
| 4 | latency 0.15 s | 0.65 | 0.15 | 0.34 m | 52.8 s | 3.09 |
| 4 | latency 0.15 s, uncompensated | 0.60 | 0.05 | 0.34 m | 55.8 s | 3.13 |
| 8 (0.28) | baseline (blind) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.02 m | 11.6 s | 3.00 |
| 8 | **cost volume** | 0.90 | **0.90** | 0.03 m | 45.6 s | 3.05 |
| 8 | latency 0.15 s | 0.90 | 0.80 | 0.07 m | 50.2 s | 2.96 |
| 8 | latency 0.15 s, uncompensated | 0.85 | 0.90 | 0.03 m | 49.9 s | 3.02 |

- **Collision rate drops sharply, and the drop scales with density**: 0.60 -> 0.05 at 0.07 ped/m^2,
  0.85 -> 0.20 at 0.14, and only 1.00 -> 0.90 at 0.28. The top density is a stress case well past
  what crowd-navigation benchmarks use; at that point a robot capped at the pedestrians' own speed
  has nowhere to go, and this is where a learned policy should be able to beat the baseline.
- **Latency degrades gracefully**: at 0.15 s the collision rate is within noise of the zero-latency
  run at every density (0.15 vs 0.05, 0.15 vs 0.20, 0.80 vs 0.90). Compensation is worth something
  only at the top density (0.80 vs 0.90 uncompensated); with 1.5 control periods of staleness there
  is little for the time shift to correct.
- **Backwards compatible**: `baseline` is `cost_volume=None`, and it reproduces Phase 1 exactly --
  6/6 static pairs, zero wall collisions, identical times.
- The price of avoidance is time: 11.6 s -> 29-54 s and roughly 2x the path length. Mean |jerk| is
  flat across variants (2.94-3.13), so the dynamic cost does not make the motion rougher.

Raw per-episode metrics: `nav/results/benchmark.csv` (300 rows).

## Phase 4: the failure mode that actually shows up is livelock, not freezing

PLAN section 7 expects the robot to stop dead at high density. What the traces show is subtler and
worse: the robot keeps moving at 0.37 m/s for the full 90 s episode, circling within a 1 m patch
about 3 m short of the goal, 33 m of path length, zero collisions, 0.8 s of "freezing" time. A
pedestrian pacing back and forth across the corridor is extrapolated at constant velocity over the
whole 2.5 s horizon, which paints a wall across the only gap; the robot backs off, the pedestrian
turns around, and the two of them repeat. A freezing-time metric alone would have called this
episode healthy, which is why `path_length` and `success` have to be read together with it.

The prescribed remedy -- soft penalty plus time discount -- is `CostVolumeConfig.confidence_decay`,
a per-step confidence in the prediction (4 pedestrians, 8 seeds, `--cv confidence_decay=...`):

| `confidence_decay` | success | collision rate | min distance | nav time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1.00 (default)** | 0.88 | **0.12** | 0.58 m | 35.2 s |
| 0.95 | 0.88 | 0.25 | 0.04 m | 28.2 s |
| 0.90 | **1.00** | 0.38 | 0.19 m | 15.3 s |
| 0.85 | 1.00 | 0.50 | 0.20 m | 16.8 s |

It is a clean trade: discounting the far end of the prediction converts timeouts into arrivals and
arrivals into contacts. The default stays at 1.0 because a collision is a safety failure and a
timeout is not, and `decay_0.9` is carried in the benchmark so both operating points are on the
record.

The two failure modes PLAN lists as already-known were not reproduced: `w_goal=15` passes the
doorway on all six Phase 1 pairs, and `decay=0.15` in `Grid.bake` leaves the robot at full speed in
open space (mean 0.50 m/s).

```bash
python -m nav.sweep --mppi w_dyn=30,60,120 --densities 4 --seeds 8
python -m nav.sweep --cv confidence_decay=1.0,0.95,0.9 --densities 4 --seeds 8
```

## Control cost: `mean_jerk` was the wrong instrument

`MPPI._control_cost` adds `1/2 u^T R u + 1/2 du^T R_d du + w_dir (1 - cos dpsi) ||v||` to the stage
cost. All five knobs (`r_v`, `r_w`, `rd_v`, `rd_w`, `w_dir`) default to 0, so the term is zero to
the last bit and every benchmark above stays comparable; `test_control_cost_is_off_by_default`
pins that.

The first attempt to tune it read `mean_jerk` off the benchmark and found nothing: 3.35 at
`rd_v=0` against 3.42 at `rd_v=60`, and raising `lam` to 3.0 to un-saturate the softmax did not
help either (3.60). That is the same flatness Phase 3 already recorded across all five variants
(2.94-3.13), and the reason is that `Metrics` finite-differences the *base pose*, which is
downstream of the Ruckig OTG in `BaseController`. The OTG re-plans to a stop at every commanded
pose, so it sets the measured jerk almost independently of what MPPI commands. A control cost sits
upstream of it and is invisible there.

Measuring the command stream `u` directly instead (4 seeds, 4 pedestrians, sfm, cost volume on):

| variant | mean abs du | mean abs d2u | mean turn per step |
|---|---|---|---|
| off | 0.436 | 0.772 | 49.4 deg |
| `rd_v=20` | 0.363 | 0.611 | 41.6 deg |
| `rd_v=60` | 0.362 | 0.594 | 41.9 deg |
| `w_dir=20` | 0.368 | 0.621 | 39.6 deg |
| `rd_v=60 w_dir=20` | 0.356 | 0.579 | 39.4 deg |
| `r_v=5 rd_v=60` | 0.370 | 0.606 | 43.8 deg |

So the term works, and `rd_v` saturates by 20 -- tripling it buys 0.017 on the second difference.
`w_dir` is the better lever on turn angle specifically, which is what it was written for. The two
compose: together they take the second difference down 25% and the per-step turn down 20%.

Defaults stay at 0. `rd_v=20, w_dir=20` is the recommended starting point, but 4 seeds at one
density is below this project's bar (Phase 3 used 20 seeds x 3 densities), and the same 4-seed
sample moved `min_ped_dist` the wrong way at `rd_v=20` (0.34 vs 0.46). Turning it on by default
needs a full benchmark first.

```bash
python -m nav.sweep --mppi rd_v=0,20,60 w_dir=0,20 --densities 2 4 8 --seeds 20
```

## The wobble: the softmax is saturated, so the robot executes one noise sample

Reported symptom: the base shakes instead of tracking smoothly. Root cause is `eta`, the number
of samples carrying non-negligible weight (`sum_k exp(-(S_k - min S)/lam)`, in `[1, K]`). Measured
at **eta ~ 1.1 out of K = 512**. The weighted update is therefore not an average at all -- each
control period executes a single raw noise draw, and consecutive draws are independent. That is
the wobble.

Four stabilisers were ported from `mppi_torch` (STORM lineage) and ablated one at a time on the
crowd-crossing episode, 4 pedestrians, 4 seeds, measuring the command stream `u` directly:

| variant | mean abs d2u | turn per step | eta | nav time | success |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none (before) | 0.725 | 43.4 deg | 1.12 | 20.2 s | 100% |
| `adapt_lam` | 0.809 | 87.8 deg | 1.71 | 40.0 s | **0%** |
| `hold_tail` | 0.758 | 48.1 deg | 1.14 | 21.7 s | 100% |
| `sgf_window=9` | **0.384** | **26.6 deg** | 1.18 | 21.3 s | 100% |
| `sample_previous_plan` | 0.786 | 49.4 deg | 1.12 | 22.1 s | 100% |
| all four | 0.145 | 13.4 deg | 10.91 | 29.9 s | 100% |
| all but `sgf_window` | 0.832 | 88.1 deg | 1.68 | 40.0 s | **0%** |

Only the Savitzky-Golay filter is on by default. It is a filter on the *solution*, not another
term in the objective, which is why it can halve the second difference without trading anything
away.

`adapt_lam` is implemented faithfully and does converge -- from `lam=1e-3` it climbs to ~8 and
then hunts around `eta` in `[5, 10]`. It is off because the band is unreachable here at any useful
speed: sample costs are spread far wider than `lam`, so `eta` stays at 1-2 all the way to
`lam = 20` and then jumps to 92 near `lam = 30`, where averaging the noise away drops commanded
speed from 0.65 to 0.15 m/s. There is no middle ground, and switching it on alone timed out every
episode. `hold_tail` and `sample_previous_plan` are principled but measured within noise, so they
stay off too. All four remain sweepable.

### Savitzky-Golay against the full benchmark

3 densities x 8 seeds, then 20 seeds at the stress density:

| pedestrians | filter | success | collisions | min dist | nav time | path | mean jerk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | off | 1.00 | 2 | 0.20 | 25.9 s | 10.88 m | 3.34 |
| 2 | on | 1.00 | 2 | 0.16 | 22.9 s | 10.38 m | 2.97 |
| 4 | off | 0.88 | 1 | 0.52 | 37.0 s | 13.12 m | 3.36 |
| 4 | on | 0.88 | 0 | 0.73 | 34.1 s | 12.98 m | 2.95 |
| 8 (20 seeds) | off | 0.85 | 31 | 0.01 | 47.3 s | 16.63 m | 3.34 |
| 8 (20 seeds) | on | 0.80 | 23 | 0.02 | 43.9 s | 16.53 m | 2.83 |

Faster and shorter at every density, with fewer total collisions (31 -> 23 at the stress density).
Success at 8 pedestrians is one episode out of 20 worse, which is not separable from noise in
either direction; that density already collides in 80% of episodes with or without the filter, so
it is not a regime this knob decides.

This is also the first change in the project to move `mean_jerk` (3.34 -> 2.83). Phase 3 recorded
it flat at 2.94-3.13 across every variant, and the control cost did not move it either, because
`Metrics` finite-differences the base pose *downstream of the Ruckig OTG*. Reranking noisy samples
is invisible there; low-frequency smoothing of the command is not.

```bash
python -m nav.sweep --mppi sgf_window=0,9 --densities 2 4 8 --seeds 8
python -m nav.sweep --mppi adapt_lam=true,false hold_tail=true,false --densities 4 --seeds 8
```
